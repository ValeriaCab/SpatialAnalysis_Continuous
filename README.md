# **The Impact of Directional Selection on Patterns of Genetic and Phenotypic Variation on a Continuous Space**

## Abstract 
The relationship between the intensity of directional selection and the phenotype of individuals in a given environment is a widely studied topic in evolutionary biology. Studying this relationship allows us to predict how populations respond to selective pressures such as climate change, the availability of different resources, and competition with other species.
However, most theoretical models do not account for the geographic context, which also influences patterns of genetic and phenotypic variation. It is necessary to consider the geographic location of individuals when studying directional selection in order to understand the dynamics of this process in spatial models with changing and heterogeneous environments. In this study, we conducted simulations in SLiM to analyze how spatially varying environmental factors and directional selection can influence phenotypic values and genetic variants that affect a specific phenotype. We hope to establish general rules regarding how gene flow, changing environmental factors, and the intensity of directional selection determine the evolution of phenotypes and genetic variants that influence complex traits.

## Description
This repository contains the simulation and analysis scripts used to study the impact of natural selection on patterns of genetic and phenotypic diversity in populations distributed across a continuous space using *forward-time* simulations implemented in SLiM.

To evaluate the adaptive response of quantitative traits, two environmental scenarios (homogeneous and heterogeneous) were compared under regimes of stabilizing and directional natural selection, as commonly described in the literature on complex traits. We characterized the distribution of mutational effects, the phenotypic mean and genetic values by region, the phenotype-genotype architecture, and the $F_{ST}$ statistic as a measure of genetic differentiation between regions
